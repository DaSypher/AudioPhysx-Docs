# Your first AudioPhysx MetaSound

This example builds an action sound with three stages: a short click, a mechanical movement and a final latch. You will trigger the entire action once, and the Sequence Wave Player will start each recording after the preceding one finishes. The goal is to understand the difference between event wiring, stereo audio wiring and completion without involving any world-based acoustic calculations.

## Assemble the graph

Create a **stereo MetaSound Source** and import three Wave Assets that you can distinguish easily. They may be mono or stereo recordings, but the AudioPhysx player will expose two channel outputs in the graph. Add `AP: Sequence Wave Player` from **AudioPhysx → Playback** and expand the `Wave` slots to three entries. Put the recordings into those slots in the desired order. The timing comes from the duration of each recording, not from a separate list of manually typed delays.

Connect the Source's `On Play` trigger to the player's `Play` input. Connect the player's `Out Left` to the stereo Source Left output and `Out Right` to the Source Right output. With `Loop Sequence` disabled, you can connect the player's `On Finished` to the MetaSound Source completion trigger when this player is the last audible stage. If you later add Early Reflections or Acoustic Paths, completion should instead account for the tail generated by the downstream processor.

Press Preview. The result should sound like one structured action: click, movement, latch. Now swap the middle recording for a much longer file. The last stage should happen later automatically because the player waits for playback completion. This is why a sequence node can be more robust than scheduling every stage with a hard-coded number of milliseconds.

## Make the design more flexible

Try turning on `Loop Sequence`. The complete three-part action should repeat rather than ending after its final clip. Turn looping off again and press `Stop` while the middle recording is audible. The stop request cancels the sequence; it is not the same thing as completing the sequence naturally, so do not assume it will issue `On Finished`.

When you want the three stages to remain in order but use different recordings on each play, move to [Random Sequence Wave Player](../metasound/playback-nodes/random-sequence-wave-player.md). When the stages should overlap instead of waiting for completion, try [Layered Wave Player](../metasound/playback-nodes/layered-wave-player.md). Both now output stereo, so the channel-wiring pattern stays familiar.

If you are building a sound that should be precisely located at a point in 3D space, the [stereo routing guide](stereo-routing.md) explains when to keep both channels and when to convert the final result to mono before spatialization.
